Night sky data and rendering
===========================

Bright stars: The Bright Star Catalogue, 5th Revised Ed. (Preliminary Version),
Dorrit Hoffleit and Wayne H. Warren Jr. (1991), CDS catalogue V/50.
https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/ftp/V/50/ReadMe
https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/ftp/V/50/catalog.gz

The local subset contains 4,103 entries with V magnitude <= 5.8 and valid
J2000 coordinates. Each row stores HR number, right ascension (degrees),
declination (degrees), V magnitude and B-V colour index (missing B-V: 0.45).
No reference star chart images are redistributed. The original catalogue
authors and the CDS astronomical data centre are acknowledged above.

Rebuild: python3 scripts/assets/build-star-catalog.py /path/to/catalog.gz

The star map uses a representative latitude of 30 degrees north and a
seasonal sidereal orientation. This preserves actual relative stellar
positions (including the Big Dipper, Cassiopeia and Orion), but is not an
ephemeris for the user's geographic position or the current calendar date.
An optional constellation guide adds labelled stick figures for the Big Dipper,
Cassiopeia, Orion, Cygnus and Lyra, plus all twelve zodiac constellations:
Aries, Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpius, Sagittarius,
Capricornus, Aquarius and Pisces. The figures use HR identifiers from this same
catalogue (src/zodiac-constellations.js). These are illustrative connecting lines, not IAU boundary polygons.

Galactic plane orientation: ICRS north Galactic pole
(RA, Dec) = (192.85948, +27.12825) degrees; ESA Gaia DR3 documentation:
https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/documentation/GDR3/Data_processing/chap_cu3ast/sec_cu3ast_intro/ssec_cu3ast_intro_tansforms.html

The Milky Way's diffuse light, dust rifts and unresolved background stars are
procedural scenery, not a survey image. Meteors and the occasional comet
are fictional visual events, not predictions of actual astronomical events.
